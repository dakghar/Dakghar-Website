## Executive Summary

### The Opportunity

India's informal retail sector represents a ₹40 trillion market, with street vendors constituting a critical last-mile distribution channel serving over 450 million urban consumers. Despite their economic significance, these 10+ million nomadic vendors operate in an analog world, relying on word-of-mouth, irregular routes, and inefficient customer discovery. Simultaneously, urban consumers waste time waiting for vendors who may never arrive, creating a massive coordination failure costing an estimated ₹50,000 crores annually in lost economic value.

Visible Chain Vyaapari solves this fundamental market inefficiency by creating India's first digital coordination platform for nomadic street vendors and their customers. We transform the chaotic, unpredictable street vendor ecosystem into an organized, transparent marketplace where vendors announce their routes in advance, customers know when to expect their regular vendors, and real-time location tracking eliminates the guesswork from informal retail.

### The Solution

Visible Chain is a two-sided mobile marketplace connecting nomadic vendors (vegetable sellers, fruit vendors, dairy suppliers, fish merchants) with customers in residential neighborhoods. Vendors use our platform to:

1. **Schedule visits** to specific localities, announcing in advance when they'll arrive
2. **Broadcast live location** so customers can track their approach in real-time
3. **Ring digital bells** when arriving at a locality, notifying interested customers instantly
4. **Build customer relationships** through consistent, predictable service

Customers use the platform to:

1. **Invite vendors** to their locality when they need specific products
2. **View schedules** to know when their preferred vendors will arrive
3. **Track nearby vendors** in real-time on an interactive map
4. **Receive notifications** when vendors ring the bell for their area

### Business Model Innovation

We've designed a sustainable, scalable revenue model based on a **coin-based economy** that aligns platform incentives with vendor success:

- **Vendors purchase coins** (virtual currency) used to access premium features
- **Dynamic pricing** adjusts costs based on real-time demand in different localities
- **Schedule announcements cost coins** (10 coins per locality, with demand surcharges)
- **Live broadcasting costs coins** (20 coins per hour, variable by area and time)
- **Verified vendors get discounts** (20% off), incentivizing quality and trust
- **New vendors receive joining bonuses** (50 coins) to reduce initial friction

This model creates natural quality controls (spam prevention through costs), generates recurring revenue (vendors broadcast daily), and scales efficiently (marginal costs approach zero).

### Market Traction Potential

Our go-to-market strategy targets **Pune first** (population 5.8M, 50+ wards), where we can:

- Capture 5% of vendors (2,500 active vendors) in Year 1
- Serve 50,000 customer households by end of Year 1  
- Generate ₹4.2 crores in annual revenue from vendor subscriptions
- Achieve positive unit economics within 6 months of launch

Success in Pune provides a replicable playbook for expansion to Mumbai, Bangalore, Delhi, and 20+ Tier-1 cities, capturing a ₹500+ crore market opportunity over five years.

### Why Now?

Four converging trends make this the right moment:

1. **Smartphone penetration**: 75% of street vendors now own smartphones (up from 35% in 2020)
2. **Digital payments adoption**: Vendors are comfortable with UPI, reducing payment friction
3. **Customer expectations**: Urban consumers expect Uber-like convenience in all services
4. **COVID acceleration**: The pandemic taught both vendors and customers to coordinate digitally

**Pain Points:**
- Wasted time visiting localities with low demand
- Inability to inform customers about schedule changes
- Lost sales when customers don't know they're in the area

- Increase daily revenue by 20-30% through better route planning
- Reduce wasted fuel/time by visiting high-demand areas
- Build loyal customer base through reliable scheduling
- Differentiate from competitors who remain analog
- Professional image appealing to younger, educated consumers

#### Secondary: Urban Customers

**Profile:**
- Urban households in Tier-1/Tier-2 cities
- Income >₹5 lakh annually household income
- Prefer fresh produce from vendors over supermarkets (quality, price, convenience)
- Currently rely on vendors but frustrated by unpredictability

**Demographic Segments:**

1. **Young Families** (30% of users)
   - Age 28-40, dual-income households
   - Time-poor, value predictability
   - Willing to pay premium for convenience
   - High smartphone usage, comfort with apps

2. **Senior Citizens** (25% of users)
   - Age 60+, retired
   - Prefer vendors for freshness and social interaction
   - Need notification systems (can't chase vendors)
   - Growing smartphone adoption in this demographic

3. **Working Professionals** (25% of users)
   - Age 25-35, single or newly married
   - Want to buy from vendors but timing never works
   - Value real-time tracking to coordinate purchases
   - Highest willingness to pay for convenience

4. **Traditional Homemakers** (20% of users)
   - Age 35-60, manage household
   - Established vendor relationships they want to maintain
   - Need reliability in vendor schedules for meal planning
   - Moderate smartphone usage

**Pain Points:**
- Vendors arrive unpredictably or skip expected visits
- Miss vendors because don't know they're nearby
- Can't plan meals or shopping because vendor schedules unknown

### Market Dynamics

#### Growth Drivers

1. **Smartphone Penetration**: India to reach 1 billion smartphones by 2026, with street vendors adopting at accelerating rates

2. **Digital Literacy**: Government's Digital India initiative has trained millions in basic smartphone usage

3. **Hyperlocal Delivery Trend**: Success of Dunzo, Swiggy Instamart, Zepto proves consumers value hyperlocal convenience

4. **Gig Economy Normalization**: Vendors increasingly view themselves as micro-entrepreneurs seeking digital tools

5. **Urbanization**: India adding 10 million urban residents annually, all needing access to street vendors

#### Market Challenges

1. **Low Technology Adoption**: Many vendors still resistant to digital tools, preferring traditional methods

2. **Network Effects Required**: Platform only valuable when sufficient vendors AND customers in each locality

3. **Language Barriers**: Need support for Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali to serve diverse markets

4. **Infrastructure Gaps**: Unreliable internet in some areas affects real-time features

5. **Payment Collection**: Vendors accustomed to cash, may resist paying for digital services

**Favorable Factors:**

- **Street Vendor Act 2014**: Central government recognizes vendor rights, promotes formalization
- **PM SVANidhi Scheme**: ₹10,000 working capital loans to vendors, encouraging professionalization  
- **Startup India Initiative**: Government support for technology solutions for informal sector
- **No licensing required**: Platform operates as technology service, not retail intermediary

**Potential Risks:**

- **Municipal regulations**: Some cities restrict vendor operating hours/locations
- **Data privacy**: Need GDPR-equivalent compliance for customer location data
- **GST implications**: Vendors earning above threshold may need registration
- **Labor classification**: Must ensure vendors remain independent, not employees

## Revenue Model & Pricing Strategy

### Coin-Based Economy

We've designed a virtual currency system that provides flexibility, simplicity, and psychological benefits:

**How It Works:**

- Vendors purchase coins in packages (100 coins = ₹50, 500 coins = ₹200, 1000 coins = ₹350)
- Coins are used to access premium features (scheduling, broadcasting)
- Coins are integers only (no decimals), simplifying mental math
- Dynamic pricing adjusts coin costs based on real-time demand

**Why Coins vs. Direct Rupee Pricing:**

1. **Psychological Decoupling**: Spending "10 coins" feels less painful than "₹5"
2. **Pricing Flexibility**: Can adjust coin packages without changing feature costs
3. **Promotional Power**: Easy to award bonus coins for referrals, loyalty
4. **Gamification**: Earning coins through good behavior (high ratings) encourages quality
5. **Transaction Efficiency**: Single coin purchase covers multiple micro-transactions

### Pricing Tiers

#### Coin Packages (Vendor Purchases)

| Package | Coins | Price (₹) | Per-Coin Cost | Discount |
|---------|-------|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Starter | 100 | 50 | ₹0.50 | 0% |
| Regular | 500 | 200 | ₹0.40 | 20% |
| Premium | 1,000 | 350 | ₹0.35 | 30% |
| Enterprise | 5,000 | 1,500 | ₹0.30 | 40% |

**Package Strategy:**

- Starter: Trial package for new vendors, low commitment
- Regular: Most common package, optimized for weekly active vendors
- Premium: Best value, targets highly active vendors
- Enterprise: For vendor cooperatives or super-users

#### Feature Costs (Coin Consumption)

**Schedule Creation:**

- **Base cost**: 10 coins per locality included
- **High-demand surcharge**: +5 coins per locality (when demand/supply > 2:1)
- **Verified discount**: -20% total cost for verified vendors
- **Example**: 3 localities, 1 high-demand = (30 + 5) × 0.8 = 28 coins for verified vendor

**Live Broadcasting:**

- **Base cost**: 20 coins per hour
- **Peak-time surcharge**: +10 coins per hour (6-10 AM, 5-9 PM)
- **Premium area surcharge**: +5 coins per hour (affluent localities)
- **Verified discount**: -20% total cost
- **Example**: 2 hours in premium area during peak time = (20 + 10 + 5) × 2 × 0.8 = 56 coins

**Promotional Offers:**

- **Joining bonus**: 50 coins free upon signup and verification
- **Referral bonus**: 25 coins when referred vendor completes first schedule
- **Rating bonus**: 10 coins monthly for maintaining 4.5+ rating
- **Loyalty bonus**: 5% extra coins on packages after ₹1,000 cumulative purchases

### Dynamic Pricing Rationale

Prices adjust based on real-time supply-demand dynamics:

**High-Demand Scenarios** (Surcharges Apply):

- More customer invites than vendor schedules (demand > supply)
- Peak times when customers most need vendors
- Affluent localities where customers have higher willingness to pay
- Special occasions (festivals, holidays) when demand spikes

**Benefits:**

1. **Vendor Incentives**: Surcharges encourage vendors to serve underserved areas
2. **Revenue Optimization**: Capture value where customers value service most
3. **Market Efficiency**: Price signals guide vendor route decisions
4. **Quality Control**: Higher costs in premium areas attract better vendors

**Low-Demand Scenarios** (Potential Discounts):

- Few customer invites, many vendor schedules (supply > demand)
- Off-peak hours or days
- Newly launched localities where we want to seed activity
- Vendor retention incentives when churn risk detected

### Unit Economics

#### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

**Vendor CAC:**

- **Organic**: ₹150/vendor (in-person demos at vendor markets, word-of-mouth)
- **Paid**: ₹400/vendor (Facebook/Google ads targeting vendor demographics)
- **Blended**: ₹250/vendor (assuming 60% organic, 40% paid)

**Customer CAC:**

- **Organic**: ₹50/customer (vendor referrals, neighborhood awareness)
- **Paid**: ₹150/customer (targeted local ads, building societies)
- **Blended**: ₹80/customer (assuming 70% organic, 30% paid)

#### Lifetime Value (LTV)

**Vendor LTV:**

- Average active vendor lifespan: 24 months
- Average monthly coin purchase: ₹200
- Gross revenue over lifetime: ₹4,800
- Platform costs (payment gateway, support): 15% = ₹720
- **Net LTV**: ₹4,080

**LTV/CAC Ratio**: ₹4,080 / ₹250 = **16.3x** (Excellent - target is >3x)

**Customer LTV:**

- Customers don't pay directly (free to use)
- Value is in attracting vendors to platform
- Measured indirectly through vendor retention (vendors stay where customers are)
- 1 active customer attracts ~0.5 vendor-months of activity
- **Indirect value**: ₹100/customer lifetime

## Go-To-Market Strategy

### Phase 1: Pune Pilot (Months 1-6)

**Why Pune First:**

1. **Manageable Scale**: 5.8M population, 50 wards - large enough to prove model, small enough to execute
2. **Tech-Savvy Population**: High smartphone penetration, educated consumers
3. **Organized Wards**: Clear administrative boundaries, accessible municipal data
4. **Vendor Density**: Thriving street vendor culture across all product categories
5. **Competitive Dynamics**: No entrenched competition, open playing field
6. **Team Proximity**: Founders based in Pune, can provide hands-on support

**Target Neighborhoods (First 5 Wards):**

1. **Kothrud**: Residential, middle-class, 120K population
2. **Shivaji Nagar**: Mixed commercial-residential, high vendor traffic
3. **Deccan**: Student population, frequent small purchases
4. **Karve Nagar**: Established families, vendor-dependent
5. **Parvati**: Dense residential, strong community bonds

**Launch Sequence:**

**Month 1: Ground Zero**

- Recruit 50 "Champion Vendors" across 5 wards through in-person demos
- Conduct vendor orientation workshops (groups of 10, teach app usage)
- Award 100 bonus coins to early adopters (₹50 value, reduces barrier)
- Target product categories: Vegetables (highest frequency, best proof of concept)

**Month 2: Seed Customer Base**

- Champion vendors invite their existing customers to app
- Door-to-door awareness in target localities (student interns, flyers)
- Partner with Residential Societies (90,000 apartments in Pune)
- Goal: 500 customers per ward = 2,500 total

**Month 3: Demonstrate Value**

- Vendors now scheduling 2-3 times/week, customers seeing notifications
- Collect testimonials from satisfied vendors and customers
- Refine pricing based on actual usage patterns
- Add 50 more vendors based on customer demand signals

**Month 4: Expand Product Categories**

- Recruit fruit vendors (30), dairy vendors (20), fish vendors (10)
- Now have coverage across all meal components
- Customers can plan complete shopping through app
- Goal: 200 active vendors, 5,000 customers

**Month 5: Iterate & Optimize**

- Analyze usage data: which features used, which ignored
- A/B test pricing models, notification timing
- Optimize vendor acquisition funnel based on CAC data
- Launch referral program (both vendors and customers)

**Month 6: Prove Unit Economics**

- Demonstrate positive contribution margin
- Achieve target: 500 active vendors, 15,000 customers across 5 wards
- Prepare case study for investor pitch and next cities
- **Key Metric**: 70% of vendors profitable (revenue > CAC) within 3 months

### Phase 2: Pune Scale-Up (Months 7-12)

- Leverage proof of concept from initial 5 wards
- Hire 3 Field Managers to cover city in zones

**Marketing Initiatives:**

2. **Local Media**: Marathi newspapers, FM radio reaching vendor demographic
3. **Society Partnerships**: MOUs with 500 residential societies
4. **Influencer Marketing**: Pune food bloggers showcasing vendor produce

**Metrics Target by Month 12:**

- 2,500 active vendors (5% of Pune's 50,000 vendors)
- 50,000 customer households (12% of Pune's 400K households)
- 75% Month-6 retention for vendors
- ₹5 lakh daily gross revenue (₹15 crore annually run-rate)

## Key Metrics & KPIs

### North Star Metric

**Successful Vendor-Customer Matches Per Day**

Measured as: Vendor rings bell → Customer receives notification → Purchase happens

This metric captures our core value proposition: coordinating vendors and customers successfully.

**Target Trajectory:**

- Month 3: 100 matches/day (Pune pilot)
- Month 6: 500 matches/day (Pune expanded)
- Month 12: 2,500 matches/day (Pune full scale)
- Month 24: 25,000 matches/day (Multi-city)

### Acquisition Metrics

**Vendor Metrics:**

- **Vendor Signups**: New registrations per week
- **Vendor Activation Rate**: % who create first schedule within 7 days
- **Target**: 70% activation rate
- **Vendor CAC**: Cost per acquired active vendor
- **Target**: ₹250 blended CAC

**Customer Metrics:**

- **Customer Signups**: New registrations per week  
- **Customer Activation Rate**: % who create first invite or view schedule within 7 days
- **Target**: 50% activation rate (customers are passive initially)
- **Customer CAC**: Cost per acquired active customer
- **Target**: ₹80 blended CAC

### Engagement Metrics

**Vendor Engagement:**

- **Weekly Active Vendors (WAV)**: Vendors who create schedule or broadcast in past 7 days
- **Target**: 60% of total vendors
- **Schedules per Active Vendor per Week**: Average number of schedules created
- **Target**: 3 schedules/week (corresponds to regular routes)
- **Broadcast Hours per Active Vendor per Week**: Average time spent live
- **Target**: 6 hours/week (2 sessions of 3 hours each)
- **Coin Purchase Frequency**: How often vendors buy coins
- **Target**: Every 2-3 weeks for active vendors

**Customer Engagement:**

- **Weekly Active Customers (WAC)**: Customers who view schedules, track vendors, or create invites
- **Target**: 40% of total customers (customers don't need daily usage)
- **Invites Created per Active Customer per Week**
- **Target**: 0.5 invites/week (1 invite every 2 weeks)
- **Vendor Tracks per Active Customer per Week**: How often customers track live vendors
- **Target**: 2 tracks/week

### Retention Metrics

**Vendor Retention:**

- **Day 1 Retention**: % vendors who return day after signup
- **Target**: 80%
- **Week 1 Retention**: % vendors active in week after signup
- **Target**: 70%
- **Month 1 Retention**: % vendors active in month after signup
- **Target**: 60%
- **Month 3 Retention**: % vendors still active after 3 months
- **Target**: 50% (sustainable cohort)

**Customer Retention:**

- **Week 1 Retention**: % customers who return in week after signup
- **Target**: 60%
- **Month 1 Retention**: % customers active after 1 month
- **Target**: 50%
- **Month 6 Retention**: % customers active after 6 months
- **Target**: 40% (long-term user base)

### Revenue Metrics

**Per-Vendor Economics:**

- **Average Revenue Per Active Vendor (ARPA)**: Monthly coin purchases
- **Target**: ₹200/month
- **Vendor Lifetime Value (LTV)**: Total revenue over vendor lifespan
- **Target**: ₹4,080 over 24 months
- **LTV/CAC Ratio**
- **Target**: >10x (currently projecting 16x)

**Platform Economics:**

- **Gross Merchandise Value (GMV)**: Total coin value sold
- **Take Rate**: Platform's % of GMV (100% - we sell coins directly)
- **Net Revenue**: GMV minus payment gateway fees (15%)
- **Target Net Margin**: 85% of GMV

**Growth Metrics:**

- **Month-over-Month Growth Rate (MoM)**
- **Target**: 30% MoM in early months, 15% MoM at scale
- **Viral Coefficient**: How many new users does each user bring?
- **Target**: 0.3 (each vendor brings 0.3 new vendors, compounds over time)
- **Payback Period**: Months to recover CAC from revenue
- **Target**: 3 months for vendors

### Operational Metrics

**Platform Health:**

- **Average Location Update Latency**: Time from vendor location broadcast to customer app update
- **Target**: <3 seconds
- **Notification Delivery Rate**: % of bell notifications delivered successfully
- **Target**: >95%
- **App Crash Rate**: % of sessions ending in crash
- **Target**: <1%
- **Customer Support Tickets per 1000 Active Users**
- **Target**: <50 tickets/month

**Quality Metrics:**

- **Vendor Verification Rate**: % of vendors who complete verification
- **Target**: 40% (verified vendors are higher quality)
- **Average Vendor Rating**: Customer ratings on 5-star scale
- **Target**: 4.2 average
- **Schedule Adherence**: % of scheduled visits that actually happen
- **Target**: 85% (vendors sometimes have legitimate reasons to cancel)

**Weekly Operations Dashboard:**

- WAV, WAC, Signups (Vendor & Customer)
- Successful Matches (North Star)
- Revenue (Daily, Weekly, Projected Monthly)
- Top Issues (Support ticket categories)

**Monthly Business Review:**

- Cohort Analysis (Retention curves by signup month)
- LTV/CAC by acquisition channel
- MoM Growth across all metrics
- City-wise performance comparison
- Product feature usage (which features drive retention?)

**Quarterly Strategic Review:**

- Market penetration (% of addressable vendors/customers)

## Risk Analysis & Mitigation

### Market Risks

**Risk 1: Low Vendor Technology Adoption**

**Severity:** High | **Probability:** Medium

**Description:**
Street vendors may be resistant to adopting smartphone apps due to low digital literacy, age demographics, or skepticism about technology value.

**Mitigation:**
- Focus initial acquisition on younger vendors (25-40 age group) with smartphones
- Provide hands-on training workshops, not just app downloads
- Design extremely simple UI with vernacular language support
- Demonstrate immediate value (free joining bonus shows instant benefit)
- Leverage peer effects (vendors see competitors succeeding)

**Risk 2: Insufficient Network Effects**

**Severity:** High | **Probability:** Medium

**Description:**
Platform only valuable when sufficient vendors AND customers in each locality. Chicken-and-egg problem harder than expected.

**Mitigation:**
- Start hyperlocal (5 wards) where we can achieve density
- Vendor-first strategy (they bring customers, not vice versa)
- Subsidize early adopters heavily (100 coins free = ₹50 value)
- Partner with existing vendor groups/associations for bulk onboarding
- Measure and optimize "match rate" (vendor schedules meeting customer invites)

**Risk 3: Competitive Response**

**Severity:** Medium | **Probability:** Low (but increasing)

**Description:**
Large players (Swiggy, Dunzo, Amazon) notice our success and launch competing features or acquire us aggressively.

**Mitigation:**
- Build strong vendor relationships (trust is barrier to entry)
- Focus on niche (nomadic vendors), avoid competing with grocery delivery
- Rapidly expand to multiple cities (harder to copy at scale)
- Develop proprietary data (routing algorithms, demand prediction)
- Be acquisition-ready if strategic buyer emerges with fair valuation

### Operational Risks

**Risk 4: Customer Safety & Trust Issues**

**Severity:** High | **Probability:** Low

**Description:**
Customers may fear inviting unknown vendors to their homes. Bad actor vendors could misuse location data, creating safety concerns.

**Mitigation:**
- Mandatory vendor verification (phone, ID proof, business license)
- Customer ratings/reviews system (bad vendors get removed)
- No home addresses shared (only locality-level data)
- Emergency contact feature if customers feel unsafe
- Partner with police/municipal authorities for vendor vetting
- Insurance coverage for any safety incidents

**Risk 5: Platform Quality & Reliability**

**Severity:** Medium | **Probability:** Medium

**Description:**
Technical issues (app crashes, location tracking failures, notification delays) erode trust in platform quality.

**Mitigation:**
- Invest heavily in engineering quality (event sourcing provides audit trail)
- Comprehensive testing before launch (beta with 50 vendors for 2 months)
- Real-time monitoring and alerting for system issues
- 24/7 support team for critical issues
- Offline-first design (app works even with poor connectivity)
- Gradual rollout (issues caught early in small cohort)

**Risk 6: Payment Collection Challenges**

**Severity:** Medium | **Probability:** High

**Description:**
Vendors accustomed to cash may resist paying for platform in coins/rupees. Payment gateway failures or fraud could hurt revenue.

**Mitigation:**
- Multiple payment options (UPI, cards, net banking, cash via agents)
- Small package sizes (₹50 starter pack reduces commitment fear)
- Clear value demonstration (free trial shows benefits before payment)
- Agent-based cash collection in early markets (convert to digital over time)
- Payment gateway redundancy (Razorpay + PayU backups)
- Fraud detection algorithms (unusual purchase patterns)

### Financial Risks

**Risk 7: Slower-Than-Expected Revenue Ramp**

**Severity:** Medium | **Probability:** Medium

**Description:**
Vendors take longer to become regular users or spend less per month than projected, extending path to profitability.

**Mitigation:**
- Conservative financial projections (assume 60% engagement, not 80%)
- Multiple pricing experiments to optimize ARPA
- Cross-sell opportunities (offer inventory management, credit in future)
- Cost discipline (outsource non-core functions, lean team)
- Raise enough capital for 18-month runway (not 12-month)

**Risk 8: Regulatory Challenges**

**Severity:** Medium | **Probability:** Low

**Description:**
Government regulations around gig economy, data privacy, or vendor licensing could increase compliance costs or restrict operations.

**Mitigation:**
- Proactive engagement with policymakers (position as vendor empowerment tool)
- Compliance with all data privacy laws (GDPR-equivalent in India)
- Vendors are independent contractors, not employees (avoid labor law complications)
- Partner with government schemes (PM SVANidhi) rather than working against them
- Legal counsel from day one to ensure all practices compliant

### Strategic Risks

**Risk 9: Feature Creep & Mission Drift**

**Severity:** Low | **Probability:** Medium

**Description:**
Temptation to add e-commerce, inventory management, credit features could distract from core coordination problem.

**Mitigation:**
- Disciplined product roadmap focused on vendor-customer coordination
- Resist feature requests that don't serve core use case
- "Do one thing exceptionally well" philosophy
- Separate teams for core vs. experimental features
- Measure feature impact on North Star metric (successful matches)

**Risk 10: Vendor Economic Hardship**

**Severity:** Medium | **Probability:** Low

**Description:**
If overall vendor incomes decline (economic recession, policy changes), they cut discretionary spending like Visible Chain subscription.

**Mitigation:**
- Low absolute costs (₹200/month sustainable even in downturn)
- Demonstrate ROI (platform increases income, not just costs)
- Flexible pricing (pause subscriptions during slow months)
- Loyalty programs (reward long-term users with discounts)
- Focus on helping vendors weather downturns (demand aggregation helps)

### Glossary of Terms

**Active Vendor:** Vendor who has created at least one schedule or gone live in the past 30 days

**Broadcast:** Vendor sharing live GPS location so customers can track their approach in real-time

**Coins:** Virtual currency vendors purchase to access premium features (schedules, broadcasting)

**Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** Total cost to acquire one active customer (marketing + sales costs)

**Dynamic Pricing:** Adjusting coin costs based on real-time demand/supply in different localities

**Event Sourcing:** Technical architecture storing all state changes as immutable events (provides audit trail)

**H3:** Hierarchical hexagonal geospatial indexing system (Uber technology for location mapping)

**Joining Bonus:** Free coins awarded to new vendors upon signup/verification (reduces initial friction)

**Lifetime Value (LTV):** Total revenue expected from one vendor over their entire time using platform

**Locality:** Neighborhood within a ward (e.g., Deccan Gymkhana within Shivajinagar Ward)

**Match:** Successful coordination where vendor schedule/broadcast connects with customer need

**Nomadic Vendor:** Street vendor who moves between multiple localities (vs. fixed-location vendors)

**North Star Metric:** Single metric best capturing core value proposition (for us: successful matches/day)

**Ring Bell:** Digital notification vendor sends upon arriving at locality (alerts interested customers)

**Schedule:** Vendor's announced plan to visit specific localities at specific times

**Verified Vendor:** Vendor who completed additional verification (ID, license) and earns trust badge

**Ward:** Administrative division in Indian cities (Pune has 50+ wards, each with 10-30 localities)

**Ward:** Visible Chain focuses on ward-level coordination since vendors typically serve 1-3 adjacent wards
